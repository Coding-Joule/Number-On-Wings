NumberOnWings: a place to poke at mathematics

Questions I keep coming back to. Tap a triangle for the real answer, not a simplified one.

Ask something

Is 0.999… equal to 1, or only very close?▼

Equal. Not nearly equal — the same number, written twice. If they differed, 1 − 0.999… would be a positive real number smaller than every 1/10ⁿ, and no such real number exists. The notation 0.999… is a name for the limit of 0.9, 0.99, 0.999, …, and that limit is exactly 1. One number is allowed two decimal names.

How many prime numbers are there?▼

Infinitely many, and Euclid proved it without finding a single new one. Take any finite list of primes, multiply them together, add 1. The result leaves a remainder of 1 when divided by every prime on your list, so either it is prime itself or it has a prime factor you never listed. Either way the list was incomplete — and it was an arbitrary list, so no finite list works.

Pick two whole numbers at random. What are the odds they share no factor?▼

6/π², about 61%. For each prime p, the chance both numbers are divisible by p is 1/p², so the chance they avoid sharing p is 1 − 1/p². Multiply that across every prime and you get 1/ζ(2). Euler showed ζ(2) = π²/6. That is why a question about factors answers with π — and why the mascot is one.

Can a ball be cut up and reassembled into two identical balls?▼

Yes — Banach–Tarski. Five pieces are enough. The catch is what "piece" means: these are not solids you could cut with anything, and they have no well-defined volume at all. Constructing them needs the axiom of choice. Nothing is created from nothing, because volume stops being a meaningful measurement partway through the argument.

Why can't you trisect an angle with straightedge and compass?▼

Because those two tools can only build lengths from rational numbers using addition, multiplication, and square roots — so every constructible number lives in a field extension whose degree is a power of 2. Trisecting 60° means solving 8x³ − 6x − 1 = 0, which has no rational root and therefore forces degree 3. Three does not divide any power of two, so the construction cannot exist.
