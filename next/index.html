Is 0.999… equal to 1, or only very close?▼

Equal. Not nearly equal — the same number, written twice. If they differed, 1 − 0.999… would be a positive real number smaller than every 1/10ⁿ, and no such real number exists. The notation 0.999… names the limit of 0.9, 0.99, 0.999, …, and that limit is exactly 1. One number is allowed two decimal names.

How many prime numbers are there?▼

Infinitely many, and Euclid proved it without finding a single new one. Take any finite list of primes, multiply them together, add 1. The result leaves remainder 1 when divided by every prime on your list, so either it is prime itself or it has a prime factor you never listed. Either way the list was incomplete — and the list was arbitrary, so no finite list can hold them all.

Pick two whole numbers at random. What are the odds they share no factor?▼

6/π², about 61%. For each prime p, the chance both numbers are divisible by p is 1/p², so the chance they avoid sharing p is 1 − 1/p². Multiply that across every prime and you get 1/ζ(2). Euler showed ζ(2) = π²/6. A question about factors answers with π — which is why the mascot is one.

Can a ball be cut up and reassembled into two identical balls?▼

Yes — Banach–Tarski. Five pieces are enough. The catch is what "piece" means: these are not shapes you could cut with anything, and they have no well-defined volume at all. Building them requires the axiom of choice. Nothing is created from nothing, because volume stops being a meaningful measurement partway through the argument.

Why can't you trisect an angle with straightedge and compass?▼

Because those two tools build lengths from rational numbers using addition, multiplication, and square roots only — so every constructible number lives in a field extension whose degree is a power of 2. Trisecting 60° means solving 8x³ − 6x − 1 = 0, which has no rational root and so forces degree 3. Three divides no power of two, so the construction cannot exist.
